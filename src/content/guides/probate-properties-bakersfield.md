# Probate Properties in Bakersfield

Inheriting a home in Bakersfield brings responsibility and complexity. Whether you're buying a probate property or selling an inherited home, understanding the process protects your interests.

## What Is a Probate Property?

A probate property is a real estate asset being sold as part of an estate settlement. Probate is the legal process by which a court validates a will, identifies assets, pays debts and taxes, and distributes the remaining property to heirs.

## The Probate Timeline in California

California probate follows a structured timeline: petition filed (weeks 1-2), court hearing (months 1-2), creditor claims period (months 3-4), sale approval (months 4-6), court confirmation (months 6-9), and escrow closes (month 9+).

Total: **9-18 months** in a straightforward case. Complex estates, disputed wills, or contested sales extend timelines significantly.

## Buying a Probate Property

**Advantages:**
- Potentially lower price due to motivated sellers
- Court oversight ensures proper legal process
- Clear title transfer

**Challenges:**
- Offers contingent on court approval
- Longer escrow (30-45 days after approval)
- Limited negotiation post-offer
- Property may need maintenance

## Selling an Inherited Home

**Steps:**
1. File probate petition if needed
2. Get appointed as executor
3. Petition court for authority to sell
4. Receive authorization and market the property
5. Accept offer and confirm with court
6. Court hearing approves sale
7. Escrow closes

**Costs:** Executor fees (3-5%), attorney fees ($2K-$8K), real estate commission (5-6%), title/escrow, court filing fees, and holding costs add up to 8-14% of sale price.

## Tax Implications

**Stepped-up basis:** Inherited property gets a "stepped-up basis" at the death date's fair market value. This massive tax advantage means you likely won't owe capital gains tax on the appreciation during the deceased's lifetime.

**No California inheritance tax:** Federal estate tax applies only to estates exceeding $14+ million.

## When to Sell vs. Hold

**Sell if:** Estate needs liquidity, heirs disagree, property is distant, maintenance costs are high, or market conditions favor sales.

**Hold if:** Property provides rental income, you plan to live in it, market appreciation is likely, or heirs want to preserve it as family property.
