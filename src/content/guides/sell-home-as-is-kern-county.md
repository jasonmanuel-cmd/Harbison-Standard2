# Sell Your Kern County Home As-Is: Fast Cash Sales Without Repairs

Life happens. Inheritance properties need quick liquidation. A home needs $30K in roof repairs you can't afford. You're relocating in 30 days and don't have time to stage a traditional sale. In these situations, selling your Kern County home as-is offers a clear path: list it as-is to a cash buyer and close fast.

This guide covers your options for selling distressed, damaged, or unwanted properties without carrying repair costs or waiting months for traditional buyers.

## Understanding "As-Is" Sales

An as-is sale means:

- **Buyer accepts the property in current condition** — no repairs, no contingencies
- **You disclose known issues** — but buyer takes property exactly as they see it
- **No inspection contingency** — buyer can't demand repairs based on inspection results
- **No appraisal contingencies** — financing falls through, deal still closes (cash buyer)
- **Faster timeline** — typically 7–30 days vs. 30–45 days traditional sale

### As-Is ≠ No Disclosure
**Critical:** As-is sales still require full property disclosure in California. You must disclose known defects, damage, past issues, and repair history. Selling as-is does NOT mean hiding problems. The buyer simply accepts those disclosed issues without demanding you fix them.

## Who Buys Kern County Homes As-Is?

Several buyer categories purchase distressed properties:

### Cash Investors & House Flippers
- Buy properties $100K–$150K below market value
- Plan to renovate and resell
- Expect 3–6 month holding period
- Make decisions in days, not weeks

### Real Estate Investment Companies
- "We Buy Houses" companies with cash ready
- Quick close, predictable process
- May offer lower prices (they assume all risk)
- Typical offer: 50–70% of market value

### Wholesalers
- Buy at steep discounts, resell to contractors
- Don't plan to renovate themselves
- Offer 40–60% of market value
- Fastest closes (3–7 days)

### Owner-Occupant Cash Buyers
- Live locally, plan to renovate as owner
- Willing to pay 70–85% market value
- Longer timeline (30 days), better price
- Most motivated by deal + location

### Rental Investors
- Buy as investment properties
- Accept issues with rent-back potential
- Pay 65–80% market value
- Longer timeline, stable offers

## Scenarios Where As-Is Sales Make Sense

### 1. Inherited Properties
You inherit your parent's home in Bakersfield. It's paid off, but you live out of state. Carrying costs (property tax, insurance, HOA) run $200–$400/month while you wait for a buyer. Selling as-is in 10 days to a cash investor means:
- Avoid 6–12 months of carrying costs
- Avoid costly repairs (roof, foundation issues)
- Close fast, redeploy capital
- Trade: Lower price (~20–25% off market)

### 2. Major Damage
Your Tehachapi rental flooded. Water damage, mold, electrical issues. Repair estimate: $25K–$40K. Selling through traditional channels, buyers will demand all repairs as condition of sale. Selling as-is:
- Avoid $30K+ repair costs
- Accept lower price ($50K–$75K less)
- Close in 2 weeks
- Net result: You keep $15K–$25K more by avoiding repairs

### 3. Probate or Estate Sales
Multiple heirs, difficulty deciding on property future. Estate needs liquidity quickly to pay taxes. As-is sale to investor:
- Eliminates family disputes (cash in hand)
- Settles estate faster
- No time wasted preparing/showing
- Probate court approves faster (asset liquidated)

### 4. Relocation Pressure
Your company relocates you to Texas in 60 days. Selling your Bakersfield home through traditional sale takes 30–45 days average; as-is takes 10–14 days:
- Hit your moving deadline
- No bridging loan needed
- Clean break, no distant landlord role
- Price trade-off: 15–20% below market

### 5. Property with Persistent Issues
Your home has chronic foundation settling, title issues, or code violations that scare traditional buyers. As-is buyer:
- Accepts these issues knowingly
- Doesn't demand city remediation
- Closes despite disclosed defects
- Avoids months of listing with no offers

## The As-Is Sales Process

### Step 1: Get Your Property Valued (1 hour)
Contact 2–3 local cash buyers for offers:
- Kern County cash investors
- Regional "We Buy Houses" companies
- Ask for no-obligation property evaluation

### Step 2: Provide Full Disclosure (1 hour)
Fill out California Property Disclosure Statement:
- List all known defects
- Disclose past repairs, damage history
- Note any pending code violations
- Attach photos of problem areas
- **This protects you legally.**

### Step 3: Negotiate Offer (1–3 days)
Cash buyer makes written offer:
- Typically 50–80% of market value
- No contingencies (as-is means as-is)
- Closing timeline specified
- Earnest money (usually waived or minimal, $1K–$5K)

### Step 4: Accept & Go to Contract (1 day)
Sign purchase agreement:
- Agreement is binding
- Specify closing date (7–30 days typical)
- Disclose final walk-through date/time
- Note: No inspection contingency
- No appraisal contingency

### Step 5: Final Walk-Through (1 day before close)
Buyer does final inspection:
- Verify property hasn't changed
- Check nothing was removed
- Accept as-is condition
- No requests for repairs (contract doesn't allow it)

### Step 6: Close (30–60 minutes)
Meet at title company:
- Sign deed
- Receive cash or wire transfer
- Keys transfer
- Done

**Total timeline: 7–14 days vs. 30–45 days traditional sale.**

## Red Flags: Scams to Avoid

Not all cash buyers are equal. Protect yourself:

### Red Flag #1: Pressure to Close Immediately
Scam: "We need to close tomorrow or offer expires."
- Legitimate: 7–14 days to close is fast
- Scam: 24–48 hours is predatory
- What to do: Walk away, find another buyer

### Red Flag #2: Lowball Offers Without Inspection
Scam: Offer without seeing property
- Legitimate: Email photos/video, then offer; verify on walk-through
- Scam: Offer $100K for $300K home sight-unseen
- What to do: Ask for verification, document discrepancy

### Red Flag #3: "We'll Handle Everything" (Title/Closing)
Scam: They provide title company, you sign what they give you
- Legitimate: You choose title company or use shared neutral
- Scam: Predatory title company, unusual closing terms
- What to do: Hire your own title company, hire lawyer for $300 review

### Red Flag #4: Hidden Costs
Scam: Offer $300K but deduct "fees" at closing
- Legitimate: Clear offer stated as cash to seller (minus title/recording)
- Scam: "Administration fee" $15K, "funding fee" $10K, etc.
- What to do: Get written offer clearly stating net proceeds

### Red Flag #5: No Earnest Money Deposit
Scam: No EMD deposit means no commitment
- Legitimate: Professional investors put down EMD ($2K–$5K) as good faith
- Scam: No EMD = no risk if they don't close
- What to do: Require EMD, hold in escrow
